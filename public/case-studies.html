01
REAL ESTATE
⌂
“We’re looking for a two-bedroom home. Could we arrange a viewing this weekend?”

Sample enquiry
Illustrative workflow

Illustrative example · outcomes to validate

A viewing request.
An agent-ready next step.

An agent receives a viewing enquiry with a budget and a rough timeframe. The property reference, contact details and preferred times still need to be collected.

THE STARTING POINT

A message in the inbox. Useful details scattered through the text. The agent starts from scratch.

THE PREPARED OUTPUT

A structured summary, a short list of missing details and a reply draft ready for review.

How the workflow is designed

  1. 01Summarise the property interest and enquiry type.
  2. 02Flag the missing details an agent needs next.
  3. 03Prepare a helpful reply for the agent to review.
◎
Where your team stays in control

The agent checks the property, availability and wording before confirming any viewing.

EXAMPLE RECORD

Status: awaiting details
Next step: ask for the property reference and preferred viewing times.

What a real pilot should measure

  • Time to prepare the first reply
  • Enquiries with complete viewing details
  • Agent time spent on repeat questions

Compare these measures against the business’s starting point before reporting any result.

02
LOCAL SERVICES
↗
“I’d like a quote for a new AC unit. Could someone come by next week?”

Sample enquiry
Illustrative workflow

Illustrative example · outcomes to validate

A quote enquiry.
A clearer scope of work.

A service business gets quote requests that leave out the address, the work involved or a suitable time to talk. The team repeats the same questions before it can assess the job.

THE STARTING POINT

A quote request without enough detail. Several replies are needed to understand the job.

THE PREPARED OUTPUT

The request is organised around the service, location and information needed before quoting.

How the workflow is designed

  1. 01Identify the service requested from the enquiry.
  2. 02Prepare a checklist of missing job details.
  3. 03Draft the next reply without inventing prices or availability.
◎
Where your team stays in control

The business reviews the work, decides whether it is a fit and prepares its own quote.

EXAMPLE RECORD

Status: awaiting job details
Next step: collect the service address and a description of the work.

What a real pilot should measure

  • Time spent gathering job details
  • Quote requests ready for assessment
  • Enquiries needing another clarification

Compare these measures against the business’s starting point before reporting any result.

03
PROFESSIONAL FIRMS
≋
“I run a small business and need help with bookkeeping. Can we arrange an initial conversation?”

Sample enquiry
Illustrative workflow

Illustrative example · outcomes to validate

A client enquiry.
A considered first response.

A professional firm needs to understand a new client’s request before proposing a consultation. Initial messages vary in detail and can arrive while the team is serving existing clients.

THE STARTING POINT

An open-ended request. Team members each interpret and summarise the message themselves.

THE PREPARED OUTPUT

A consistent intake summary and suggested next questions, ready for a professional to review.

How the workflow is designed

  1. 01Prepare a concise overview of the support requested.
  2. 02Identify the background needed for an initial conversation.
  3. 03Draft a response that keeps sensitive documents out of the first enquiry.
◎
Where your team stays in control

The team checks service suitability, wording and the appropriate next step before making commitments.

EXAMPLE RECORD

Status: needs team review
Next step: check service suitability before proposing a consultation.

What a real pilot should measure

  • Time spent preparing intake summaries
  • Enquiries ready for a suitability review
  • Clarity of information at the first conversation

Compare these measures against the business’s starting point before reporting any result.
